Chapter Document: All assets transferred to Canis.

‘Master….’

He didn’t think of Arcane as secretly warm or caring. The man had been unapologetically firm in his beliefs, so his treatment of Canis had likely been equally honest.

Arcane proved it with his will. Though he had seemed indifferent to his disciple’s life, he had considered Canis his only student.

Setting aside the will, Canis checked the asset list.

Real estate, including dungeons, magical equipment, expensive artifacts, rare herbs, and bank deposits were neatly itemized.

‘1, 10, 100, thousand, ten thousand….’

Canis counted the total at the end.

‘4.8 billion gold….’

An amount befitting the wealth accumulated by the great mage Arcane over 150 years. Yet Canis couldn’t comprehend the current situation. First, they were suddenly acquitted, and now he was the heir to a 4.8-billion-gold fortune.

Sakiri scratched his head awkwardly.

“But there’s a slight problem. Arcane left his assets to you, clearly acknowledging you as his disciple. But the Association sees brainwashing as implanting a false self. In the end, you were deceived by Arcane. Otherwise, how could you commit such atrocities? That’s why the Association is considering acquittal. But if that happens….”

“I can’t inherit? Because I’m no longer his disciple?”

Sakiri smiled warmly for the first time. Of course, his Awakened form didn’t change a whit.

“Yes. Exactly. Arcane’s heir is the brainwashed Canis, not the un-brainwashed one. The money would be seized as illegal funds and returned to the Association. So before that, we wanted your thoughts. Admit to being brainwashed, and you’ll be acquitted. Just sign here.”

Sakiri slid another document forward. Canis stared at it blankly.

A waiver of inheritance.

Admitting to brainwashing would acquit them. They were merely Arcane’s victims. But the fortune would end up in someone else’s hands.

In the end, the Association had chosen 4.8 billion gold over two insignificant kids.

“I can give you time, but it might hurt your case. It could imply voluntary crime. Honestly, does it make sense? Of course you were brainwashed. Why else would you commit such heinous acts?”

Canis’s mind went blank. He wasn’t afraid to fight and die. But the inspector said they were innocent. He had clearly done wrong, yet it was as if he hadn’t.

‘This place is no different from Radum.’

The outside world wasn’t a hothouse bathed in sunlight either. It was a battlefield where colossal desires swirled under the guise of law.

As Canis hesitated, Sakiri clicked his tongue and stood.

“Then I’ll give you some time. About an hour—”

“I’ll sign.”

Sakiri, halfway up, paused. Then he smiled and sat back down.

“A wise choice. Here….”

He handed over his own fountain pen. Canis signed the waiver.

The pen scratched against the paper. Sakiri took it back with a pleased smile and checked the document.

“Congratulations on your freedom. Now that it’s over, let me say you made the right choice. Hiring a lawyer would have complicated things.”

A lawyer. Canis hadn’t even considered it. Maybe he should have.

No, Sakiri wasn’t lying.

If the Magic Association had been lenient, they wouldn’t have presented the waiver in the first place.

“What do we do now?”

“Hmm. Well, you can leave. You’re free.”

Canis and Arin had been thoroughly outplayed. But Sakiri, as if it were none of his concern, gathered the documents and turned away.

“Where do we go for the release procedures….”

“There aren’t any. Just leave.”

The dismissive attitude made Canis realize the truth.

From the start, the Magic Association hadn’t cared about them. They had only spared two hours to reclaim Arcane’s wealth.

It wasn’t just his pride that was wounded. The Association’s overwhelming power, which he could do nothing about, was far more humiliating. It made him realize just how extraordinary Arcane had been to stand against nations.

Canis regained his composure. It was clear now. He was a battle mage. And knowing how to fight, he had no more hesitation.

“Hey, you.”

Sakiri turned at the door.

“Yes?”

“You’re a mage, right?”

“Hmm, you could say that. All Association staff are mages.”

“What’s your rank?”

“Certified 5th class, why?”

Canis gauged his opponent’s level. An inspector of the Magic Association was a 5th-class mage. That was the kind of man he was.

“This isn’t the end for me. I’ll never stay down.”

“I see. As you should. Well, I’ll be going.”

“Someday… I’ll make you my subordinate.”

Sakiri’s hand on the doorknob froze. After a moment’s thought, he turned back with a thin smile.

“You’ll have to study hard. This position is highly competitive. But if by some chance that day comes….”

Sakiri placed his right hand over his heart.

“I’ll be in your care.”

Canis didn’t react to the inspector’s uncharacteristic gesture. He only realized how thoroughly pragmatic Sakiri was. Bowing cost nothing. The future was anyone’s guess.

As Sakiri left, Arin smiled at Canis. At least they were free. Wasn’t that better than rotting in prison with 4.8 billion gold?

“Canis, you made the right choice.”

“There are… so many strong people.”

Canis’s lips curled faintly. With a new goal, his heart pounded as fiercely as when he had first met Arcane.

“Arin, let’s get stronger.”

“Yeah….”

Arin looked at the door Sakiri had exited. Canis would return here someday. Since this wasn’t their end, she too could move forward.

***

Leaving the interrogation room, Sakiri headed straight for the Inspector’s Office. The matter had been resolved easily given its gravity. Arcane’s death had been pivotal. There were no legal conflicts, and Alpheas’s admission of guilt had helped.

The Inspector’s Office was an elegant room with marble floors. A man known as the Iron Inspector, a legend of Inspection Division 3, stood with his hands behind his back, gazing out the window.

“Inspector, the approval documents for this case.”

“Leave them on the desk.”

Sakiri placed the file on the desk. Normally, he would leave, but this time curiosity got the better of him, and he turned at the door.

“Is this really alright for the Alpheas case?”

“Why? Do you think there’ll be complaints from above?”

“A six-month suspension is lenient. But I doubt there’ll be complaints. It’s a 40-year-old case with no intent to harm, and converting 4.8 billion gold into bail seems fair.”

“Then why ask?”

The question implied things were proceeding as expected. Sakiri regretted stirring trouble but couldn’t backtrack now.

“I wondered if there was an element of atonement.”

The inspector was silent for a long time. Then he slowly turned to face Sakiri.

“I did feel guilty toward Alpheas. But I’ve never let personal feelings dictate a criminal’s punishment.”

“My apologies. That was out of line.”

“You may leave.”

Flustered, Sakiri hurried out. Though infamous among criminals, within the Association, he was just another mage wary of his superior.

The inspector opened the file. After carefully reviewing the contents, he took out a fountain pen and brought it to the approval line. Countless memories flashed through his mind. Now a white-haired old man, recalling those days made his jaw ache where Alpheas had struck him.

“Tch. I should’ve brought a gift.”

After the incident with Erina, he had never visited Alpheas again, but he had lived refusing to accept his defeat.

Yet over 40 years, photon theory had evolved and revolutionized, continuing to change the world even now.

He could no longer deny how genius Alpheas’s achievements had been.

“Heh. A belated gift. The Gold Circle was rightfully yours.”

The inspector inscribed his signature on the document.

Ardiano Sarov.
